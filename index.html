Meet the founder

Hi, I’m Bijan

Half Persian, half English, and happiest somewhere I’ve never been before.

At 16 my dad took me to Iran to learn Farsi, and it changed how I saw the world. Years later, after a decade of long hours in London, my partner Ana and I swapped the city for her family’s mountain village in Romania. I built a house, ran a guesthouse, learned Romanian, and spent my weekends getting lost in the Carpathians in an old 4x4.

I’ve worked as a product designer for most of my career, obsessing over the little details that make things feel right for people. Somewhere along the way, travel and photography took me to 115 countries. My mum used to dream of seeing the world with her Polaroid camera, and I like to think I’m doing a bit of that for her.

Ana and I started Moroccan Ventures together, and now split our time between Romania and Morocco. Global Venturers is me putting all of that into small trips with people who love travel as much as I do. Come along.

The Morocco we love, shared around one table

After years of running private trips across Morocco with Moroccan Ventures, we created this journey for travellers who want to see the country beautifully and without rushing: elegant boutique riads, a luxury tented camp in the dunes, expert local guides, and our founder Bijan as your tour leader from the first evening to the last.

Places we visit along the way

  1. Settle into your boutique riad in the medina and meet your tour leader over mint tea in the courtyard. In the evening, a welcome dinner on a rooftop terrace as the city lights up.

    Overnight: Boutique riad, Marrakech

  2. An unhurried private tour of Jardin Majorelle and the Bahia Palace, then the souks with a local expert. Afternoon at leisure, with a traditional hammam ritual and time by the riad pool.

    Overnight: Boutique riad, Marrakech

  3. A scenic drive over the High Atlas by the Tizi n'Tichka pass, with time to wander the ksar of Aït Ben Haddou. Then on past Ouarzazate into the Draa Valley to Agdz, a quiet oasis town of palm groves and old kasbahs.

    Overnight: Kasbah guesthouse, Agdz

  4. Follow the Draa Valley south through Zagora to M’Hamid, the last village before the desert. From here we leave the road by 4x4 for Erg Chegaga, Morocco’s wildest dunes, arriving at camp in time for sunset.

    Overnight: Luxury desert camp, Erg Chegaga

  5. Sunrise from the highest dunes, a slow day at camp and an evening of music around the fire under a sky full of stars. Far from the crowds, this is the Sahara at its most peaceful.

    Overnight: Luxury desert camp, Erg Chegaga

  6. Back to M’Hamid by 4x4, then east on quiet desert roads through Tazzarine and Alnif, past the foot of the Jebel Saghro, to the golden dunes of Erg Chebbi at Merzouga, arriving in time for sunset over the dunes. A long day through landscapes few travellers ever see.

    Overnight: Luxury desert camp with en-suite tents, Merzouga

  7. Up the palm-lined Ziz Valley and the Ziz Gorges, then across the Middle Atlas via Midelt and the cedar forests around Ifrane, with relaxed stops along the way. Arrive in Fes in time for an evening walk in the medina.

    Overnight: Elegant riad, Fes

  8. A morning in Fes el-Bali with a historian guide: the Chouara tannery, Al Quaraouiyine and the artisans' quarters. In the afternoon, a drive into the Rif Mountains to Chefchaouen for sunset from the Spanish Mosque.

    Overnight: Boutique riad, Chefchaouen

  9. A slow morning in the blue lanes of Chefchaouen, then a short drive to Tetouan, whose UNESCO-listed medina, shaped by Andalusian refugees, is one of the best preserved and least visited in Morocco.

    Overnight: Traditional riad, Tetouan

  10. A short drive to Tangier and Cap Spartel, where the Atlantic meets the Mediterranean. Wander the kasbah and end the day with mint tea at Café Hafa as the sun sets over the Strait of Gibraltar.

    Overnight: Boutique hotel, Tangier

  11. A comfortable drive south along the Atlantic coast, with a stop in Asilah, a whitewashed seaside town of murals and ramparts. In Casablanca, visit the Hassan II Mosque, standing right on the Atlantic, and spend a last evening together by the ocean.

    Overnight: Boutique hotel, Casablanca

  12. A leisurely breakfast, then a private transfer back to Marrakech, about three hours away, in time for flights home.

Who is this journey for?

Travellers who appreciate comfort, beautiful places to stay and a relaxed pace, mostly aged 30 to 65. You’ll need a reasonable level of fitness for medina walks, and there are a few longer scenic drives with comfortable stops along the way.

No, so you can fly from wherever suits you. The journey starts and ends in Marrakech, so a simple return flight works: arrive on 10 October and fly home on 21 October. Airport transfers on those dates are included.
