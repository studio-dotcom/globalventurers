12 Days in Morocco: An Itinerary That Skips the Coach Crowds

Marrakech to the Sahara, Fes to the blue city and on to Tangier. Day by day, with the driving times nobody else tells you.

By Bijan Paul, founder & tour leader
Updated 31 October 2026 · 7 min read
The short answer

The best 12-day Morocco itinerary is a loop: Marrakech (1–2 nights), over the High Atlas via Aït Ben Haddou to the Draa Valley (1 night), two nights in the Sahara dunes, then north to Fes (1–2 nights), Chefchaouen (1 night), Tetouan or the north coast (1 night), Tangier (2 nights) and back via Casablanca. It covers the desert, the imperial cities and the north without retracing your steps.

I’ve tweaked this route more times than I can count. Over 15 years of planning trips around Morocco, this is the one I keep coming back to: it gets you to the deep desert, the old imperial cities and the quiet north, it never doubles back on itself, and nobody spends nine hours in a car two days running. It’s also, not by coincidence, the route of our own 12-day Morocco group tour. Here it is, day by day.

You’ll notice it isn’t rushed. That’s deliberate. I like to take my time and really appreciate a place, ideally with a good group of people, and you feel the beauty of Morocco so much more that way than ticking things off from a coach window.

The route at a glance

  • Day 1: Marrakech
  • Day 2: Over the High Atlas to Agdz
  • Days 3–4: Erg Chegaga (Sahara)
  • Day 5: Merzouga
  • Day 6: Fes
  • Day 7: Chefchaouen
  • Day 8: Tetouan
  • Days 9–10: Tangier and the north coast
  • Day 11: Casablanca
  • Day 12: Back to Marrakech

Day 1: Arrive in Marrakech

Land, check into a riad in the medina and do absolutely nothing ambitious. Mint tea in the courtyard. An afternoon wander through Jardin Majorelle and the souks with a local guide, who will save you from both getting lost and buying three rugs. Dinner on a rooftop as the call to prayer rolls across the city. That’s a good first day.

Tip: if you can, arrive a day early. Marrakech deserves two nights and you’ll start the road trip properly rested.

Day 2: Over the High Atlas to Agdz

Driving: around 6 to 7 hours with stops.

Up over the Tizi n’Tichka pass, more than 2,200 metres high, with switchbacks that make you very grateful you’re not driving. Stop at Aït Ben Haddou, the UNESCO-listed earthen ksar you’ve seen in films, then carry on past Ouarzazate into the Draa Valley. Agdz is a quiet oasis town with palm groves and old kasbahs. Most tours drive straight past. Don’t.

Day 3: Into the deep Sahara, Erg Chegaga

Driving: around 3 hours to M’Hamid, then about 2 hours off-road by 4x4.

Follow the Draa south through Zagora to M’Hamid, the last village before the sand. Here the road ends and the real journey starts: 4x4s across the desert to Erg Chegaga, Morocco’s wildest dunes. Arrive in time for sunset. No coaches, no tarmac, no queue of camels. Just you, the dunes and a very good dinner.

Day 4: A full day in the dunes

Sunrise from the top of a dune. A slow breakfast. Walk, nap, read, take far too many photos. In the evening, music around the fire and more stars than you’ve ever seen. This is why I always recommend two nights in the desert: the first night you arrive, the second night you actually feel it.

Day 5: Erg Chegaga to Merzouga

Driving: a long day, around 8 to 9 hours including the off-road section.

Back to M’Hamid by 4x4 and then east on quiet desert roads to Merzouga and the golden dunes of Erg Chebbi, the highest in Morocco. It’s a big driving day, but through landscapes very few travellers see, and you arrive for sunset over a completely different sea of sand. Want to know why we do both dunes? My Sahara guide explains.

Day 6: Merzouga to Fes

Driving: around 7 to 8 hours with stops.

North up the palm-lined Ziz Valley and through the Ziz Gorges, then over the Middle Atlas to Fes. Arrive in time for an evening walk in the medina. If you have an extra day in your trip, this is where I’d add it: two nights in Fes is better than one.

Day 7: Fes medina, then Chefchaouen

Driving: around 3.5 to 4 hours.

A morning in Fes el-Bali with a historian guide. It’s the largest car-free urban area in the world, and without a guide you will be lost within four minutes. See the Chouara tannery (accept the mint sprig), the doorway of Al Quaraouiyine, founded in 859 and often called the world’s oldest university, and the artisans’ quarters. After lunch, drive into the Rif Mountains to Chefchaouen and walk up to the Spanish Mosque for sunset over the blue town.

Day 8: Chefchaouen to Tetouan

Driving: around 1.5 hours.

Take your time in Chefchaouen in the morning, before the day-trippers arrive. Then a short drive to Tetouan, whose white medina was shaped by Andalusian refugees and is a UNESCO World Heritage site. It’s one of the best preserved medinas in Morocco and one of the least visited. A proper hidden gem, and a nice break from the crowds.

Day 9: Tetouan to Tangier

Driving: around 1 hour.

On to Tangier and Cap Spartel, where the Atlantic meets the Mediterranean. Wander the kasbah and finish with mint tea at Café Hafa, which has been serving it on terraces over the Strait of Gibraltar since 1921, as the sun goes down behind Spain.

Day 10: Tangier and the hidden beaches

A slower day. In the morning, drive along the coast to the quiet beaches of Dalia and Playa Belyounech, looking straight across to Spain. Very few travellers make it out here. In the afternoon, wander the medina and the Grand Socco, then a long dinner overlooking the bay.

Day 11: Down the coast to Casablanca

Driving: around 3.5 hours by road, or about 2 hours on the high-speed train.

Visit the Hassan II Mosque, built right over the Atlantic and one of the few mosques in Morocco open to non-Muslims on guided tours. Spend the last evening together by the ocean.

Day 12: Back to Marrakech

A slow breakfast and a transfer back to Marrakech (around 3 hours) for flights home. Or fly out of Casablanca if that suits you better.

How to tweak this itinerary

  • Only 10 days? Drop Merzouga and drive from Erg Chegaga back towards the Dades Valley and on to Fes, or drop Tetouan and Casablanca and fly home from Tangier.
  • 14 days? Add a second night in Marrakech at the start and a second night in Fes. Best upgrade you can make.
  • Want the beach? Swap the Casablanca night for two nights in Essaouira, about three hours from Marrakech.
  • Love walking? Add two nights in the High Atlas before heading south.
  • Visiting in summer? Shorten the desert and spend longer in the north and on the coast. The best time to visit Morocco guide explains why.

Getting around on this route

This itinerary really needs a private driver, at least from Marrakech to Fes. There’s no train to the desert, buses don’t go to Erg Chegaga, and the off-road section needs proper 4x4s and drivers who know the desert.

Organising drivers, 4x4s, desert camps and riads in nine different places is a lot of work, and it’s exactly what a small group tour takes off your hands. Have a read of my guide to small group tours of Morocco to see if it suits you.

Where to stay

Riads in the medinas of Marrakech, Fes, Chefchaouen and Tetouan, a kasbah guesthouse in Agdz, small camps in the desert and boutique hotels in Tangier and Casablanca. Avoid big chain hotels on this route if you can. They’re fine, but they could be anywhere. My guide to riads in Morocco has tips on picking a good one.

Frequently asked questions

Is 12 days enough for Morocco?

Yes, 12 days is a great length for a first trip. It’s enough for Marrakech, the High Atlas, two nights in the Sahara, Fes, Chefchaouen and the north, with a sensible pace. You won’t see everything, but nobody does, and you won’t spend every day in a car.

Should I start my Morocco trip in Marrakech or Casablanca?

Marrakech for most people. It has more direct flights, it’s the natural start of the route south to the desert, and it’s a much nicer first night. Casablanca works well as an end point, or if flights there are a lot cheaper.

Can I do this Morocco itinerary by train?

Partly. Trains connect Marrakech, Casablanca, Rabat, Fes and Tangier, and the high-speed line from Tangier to Casablanca is excellent. But there’s no train to the Sahara, the Draa Valley or Chefchaouen, so you’ll need a driver or buses for those parts.

Is it safe to self-drive in Morocco?

It’s possible, but Moroccan driving takes some getting used to: mountain switchbacks, police checkpoints, animals and some creative overtaking. The desert tracks to Erg Chegaga need an experienced 4x4 driver. You’ll enjoy the views far more if someone else is driving, which is why we use private drivers who know these roads inside out.

Can I add Essaouira to a 12-day Morocco itinerary?

You can, but something has to give. The easiest swap is to drop Tetouan or one night in Tangier and add two nights in Essaouira at the end, which is about three hours from Marrakech.

How much does a 12-day trip to Morocco cost?

For comfortable travel with boutique riads, a good desert camp and a private driver, expect roughly £3,000 to £4,500 per person for 12 days, excluding flights. A small group tour is usually cheaper than doing the same route privately, because the driver, vehicles and guides are shared.
